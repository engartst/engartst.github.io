About the Conference

The iSchools Organization Biannual Regional Mini-Conference brings together academics (students and faculty) from North American iSchools and information science practitioners to explore the current and future role of iSchools in 2025 and beyond. This two-day event focuses on collaborative knowledge sharing, innovative research presentation, and strategic planning for the future of information science education.

Academic Collaboration

Connect with faculty and students from iSchools across North America to foster inter-institutional partnerships.

Research & Innovation

Discover cutting-edge research in information science and explore innovative approaches to education and practice.

Professional Development

Engage with diverse scholarly communication formats and contribute to the future direction of iSchools.

Community Impact

Explore how iSchools can champion social justice through data equity, information literacy, and activist librarianship.

Our Commitment to Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion

We are committed to creating an inclusive and welcoming environment for all participants, regardless of race, ethnicity, gender identity, sexual orientation, age, disability, religion, or socioeconomic background. Our conference themes of data equity, social justice, and activist librarianship reflect our dedication to addressing systemic inequities in information systems and promoting equitable access to knowledge.

Accessibility & Inclusion Support

  • Physical Accessibility: Quiet spaces designated for privacy and noise-free conversations, comfortable seating arrangements in open spaces, and full ADA compliance throughout the venue.
  • Communication Access: High-quality live captions for all sessions and professionally transcribed session recordings with accurate captions for long-term accessibility.
  • Presentation Support: Speakers are encouraged to prepare accessible slides using our accessibility guidelines and presentation templates to ensure inclusive content delivery.
  • Language Support: Pre-submission review service available to help non-native English speakers refine their abstracts and presentation materials with volunteer mentors.
  • Financial Assistance: Limited financial support available for registration fees, travel, and accommodation expenses. Apply for support by August 1st.
  • Bias Mitigation: Our review process employs structured evaluation criteria and diverse review panels to minimize implicit bias and ensure equitable assessment of all submissions.

We actively encourage participation from underrepresented communities and strive to amplify diverse voices in information science. All attendees are expected to contribute to a respectful, collaborative environment that values different perspectives and experiences.

Featured Speakers

Learn from leading experts in data equity, information literacy, and activist librarianship

Dr. Tracie D. Hall

Keynote Speaker - Former Executive Director, American Library Association

Renowned advocate for intellectual freedom and social justice in libraries. Dr. Hall addresses the challenges facing modern librarians and how to prepare "warrior librarians" for the future.

Luzita Francis, MHA, MSIS

Data Equity Researcher & Public Health Advocate

Expert in health data equity examining systemic flaws in data collection that impact marginalized communities. Her research on COVID-19 mortality data reveals how institutional neglect compromises public health responses.

Dr. Jazmyne McNeese

Senior Data Analyst at PolicyMap | Community Development Enthusiast

Specialist in spatial data visualization and social inequality mapping. Dr. McNeese leads innovative approaches to making patterns of inequity visible and actionable through data-driven storytelling.

Dr. Marijel "Maggie" Melo

Associate Professor, UNC-Chapel Hill

Researcher in inclusive library and makerspace design. Dr. Melo's work focuses on how data collection practices can create more equitable environments and challenge dominant narratives about library access.
